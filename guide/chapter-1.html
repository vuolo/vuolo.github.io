Make It Exist First · Chapter 1

The number is zero, and that is the point

A free sample from a guide in draft. November 24, 2026.

I am writing this with a YouTube channel that is a few days old, a personal site that just went up, and a side-business ledger that still says $0. I am not going to tell you I have been a public creator for years, or that a team of happy clients is waiting just off-camera. I have been a software engineer for longer than I have been a person with a channel. The work behind the offers is real. The audience is not, not yet.

That is an uncomfortable sentence to put at the front of a book. It is also the only honest place to start. A lot of the internet right now is in the business of looking established. Clean type, a calm voice, a product ladder, a consulting form. Those things are useful, and I am using them. They are not a track record. A track record is a number you can show, a name you are allowed to use, a thing a stranger can click and see for themselves.

I have a motto I actually use: make it exist first, make it better later. It is not a license to ship slop. It is a rule against waiting for a perfect origin story. The first version of a channel, a tool, or a guide should be true, usable, and a little embarrassing in its smallness. The embarrassment is information. It tells you what to make better. A backdated archive, a bought following, or a testimonial you wrote for a client who does not exist teaches you nothing, and it will not survive a single curious reader.

What this guide is

This is a short book for people who already know how to build things. Engineers, operators, technical founders. People who want to run a side business with AI agents without lying to an audience, a customer, or themselves. It is about a weekly cadence, a public ledger, and a ladder of offers that starts with something free and climbs only as far as the work can support.

It is not a promise that you will make $1,500 a month by month twelve. I have modeled those odds for my own channel, and the honest median for a new face-and-voice channel, used only as a media business, is still $0 in ads a year from now. The path with a real chance is using the channel and a site like this one to bring in work I can already do: MCP servers, automations, migrations, integration health checks. Even that path is a minority outcome, not a plan you can take to a bank.

If you need a guru voice, this is the wrong book. If you want a working method for shipping in public with agents, and for knowing when to kill a bet, stay.

The three places people fake it

I have been watching creators who do this well, and a few who used to. The ones who look established after a long time usually have three habits in common, and they are not the habits you would get from a “look bigger in thirty days” thread.

They have one face. Farzad can post a lot of AI-assisted video; the risk is that the face people came for gets diluted. A personal channel should sound like you. Faceless volume, if you do it at all, belongs on a different name. I am keeping @mike-vuolo as a hub for my face and voice. That is a design choice, not a growth hack.

They show the work, including the misses. Marc Lou puts $0 products on the same page as the ones that make $40,000 a month. Pieter Levels keeps a public list of projects and says most of them failed. You do not need their numbers to copy the habit. You need a page where the status of each thing is true: prototype, waitlist, live offer, killed.

They sell what they already know how to deliver. Consulting and software come after proof, or they come as a scoped offer with a written warranty, not as a vague “I can transform your business.” A $15,000 call is a product for someone whose advice is already expensive because of a long public record. Pricing like that without the record is not ambition. It is costume.

What I am actually doing

I run side businesses with a team of AI agents: research, drafts, landing pages, checklists, the unglamorous parts of shipping every week. I still record the videos, approve anything that goes out under my name, and do the engineering that has to be right. Tests, payouts, migrations. The parts where a confident sentence is not a substitute for a passing suite.

The first video on the channel is about that arrangement, and about the $0. If the number moves, I will say so, in the same place I said it was zero. If a bet dies, I will write down why. That is the whole method, shrunk to a paragraph. The rest of this guide is the operating detail: how to pick a weekly hook, how to keep faceless experiments off your name, how to price a ladder you can defend, and how to know when you are polishing a brand instead of making the thing exist.

A note on looking refined

Typography, a consistent palette, a wordmark, a site that does not shout: these help a stranger take the next click. They are cheap, and they should be good. They should not pretend there is a roster or a ten-year archive that is not there. If you take only one thing from this chapter, take that. The fastest way to look like you have been doing this for a while is to do the next week, in public, and then do it again.
